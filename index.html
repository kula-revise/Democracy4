The essay so far

Neither side could win, but a stalemate does not negotiate itself: leaders had to choose to act on it, and in 1990 De Klerk and Mandela did. Between 1990 and 1992 both sides gave up things nobody could make them give up, which is what political will looks like in practice. But violence repeatedly did what it was meant to do and stopped the talks, so will alone cannot explain the outcome. What settled it was that after each atrocity the leaders came back, and made compromises like the sunset clause that cost them with their own supporters.

Four sentences, and together they are a complete answer to an essay question that asks to what extent. You have conceded, and you have still landed a verdict. Lesson 5 turns this into a full essay against the real memo.
